Digital NV vs Thermal — the one thing to understand first

Digital NVAmplifies existing light to make a recognizable image. You can identify your target — read markings, tell buck from doe, confirm it's not a person. Needs some ambient light or an IR illuminator. No export restrictions, no fragile tube.
ThermalDetects heat, not light. Detects game through brush, fog, and total darkness where NV sees nothing — but shows a heat blob with less fine detail, so identification is harder. Works in zero light.
DetectionHow far you can tell "something's there." Thermal wins on raw detection distance.
IdentificationHow far you can tell what it is. Digital NV and higher-res sensors win here.

The honest short version: thermal finds things; night vision identifies them. Serious night hunters often run both — thermal to scan and detect, NV or a good day scope to confirm and shoot. If you can only buy one, the right pick depends on whether your problem is finding game in cover (thermal) or identifying and shooting at known ranges (digital NV). Legal note: night-hunting and night-optic laws vary by state and by species — it is your responsibility to confirm what's legal where you hunt before buying or using these.
